\begin{abstract}
Forecasts of GRACE/GRACE-FO terrestrial water storage anomalies (TWSA) are
routinely benchmarked against persistence. We show that for basin-average,
deseasonalized TWSA this benchmark leaves the persistence class's own
headroom unexploited: the observations are noisy, and persistence propagates
the anchor month's noise into the
forecast. A per-basin AR(1)-plus-observation-noise state-space model -- a
three-parameter Kalman filter, not a data-assimilation system -- filters the
series before extrapolating. On 234 global basins over five
expanding-window test folds (issue months June 2019 onward, 83 at lead 1
shrinking to 78 at lead 6 as the shifted target runs off the May 2026 record
end), this filter beats
% source: results/paper_baseline_ladder.csv, kalman_ar1 rows h1-h2 (skill 0.0498, 0.0879)
damped persistence by 5.0\,\% and 8.8\,\% at leads 1--2 and per-basin ridge
% source: results/paper_baseline_contrasts.csv, kalman_ar1 vs ridge_own_perbasin (h4 +1.04, p=0.078 ns; others p<=0.013)
regression at five of six leads (Diebold--Mariano $p \le 0.013$; lead 4 is not
significant, $p = 0.078$). An ablation identifies the mechanism: with the
observation-noise variance forced to zero the same model class falls behind
damped persistence at leads 2--6, so the margin is the noise-filtering term
itself, not the autoregressive structure. Much of the
published skill of learned models over persistence is therefore attainable by
three-parameter state-space filtering alone. Exogenous forcing is what beats this benchmark:
% source: results/phase6_era5_headline.csv, ridge_own_era5 vs ridge_own h1 (+4.62%, p=1.0e-4); h2 +1.39% p=0.092 ns
ERA5-Land predictors add 4.6\,\% at lead 1 and nothing detectable beyond it,
% source: results/phase7_corrected_analysis.md Q1 (2-seed ensemble +2.17/+2.36/+1.43)
while sequence modeling of the forcing history adds a further 1.4--2.4\,\% at
leads 1--3. A head-to-head comparison
with the GRACE-FCast system of Li and Kusche (2026) on 227 matched basins
% source: results/phase8b_li_comparison_headline.csv, lstmres_corr_top1_ens vs li_lstm_full
locates the classic initial-conditions-versus-forcing crossover on
satellite-observed TWSA: our filtering-based system wins lead 1 by 20\,\%, the
systems are not detectably different at lead 2, and their forcing-driven system wins leads 3--6 by
13--30\,\%. Finally, we characterize the cross-basin signal in observed GRACE TWSA
% source: results/phase3b_predictions.csv via post_rerun_audit.md sect. 2 (kalman_corr_top1 vs kalman_own_ridge h1 +0.308%, p=0.199)
under null-model controls. At the linear tier it is a null result: one
correlation-selected neighbor adds $+0.31\,\%$ at lead 1 ($p = 0.199$) and
nothing at longer leads. Stratifying by the share of each basin's
% source: results/resolution_cross_2x2.csv (resolved x cont_high +1.161% p=0.0045; resolved x cont_lowmid -0.500% p=0.082)
mascon-weighted signal that comes from land outside the basin places what
pooled effect there is entirely in shared-footprint basins
($+1.16\,\%$, $p = 0.0045$, among those large enough for GRACE
to resolve; $-0.50\,\%$ elsewhere) -- a leakage signature, independent of
basin size. Delivered instead as a dedicated
residual-correction stage on a deep sequence model, the same neighbor signal adds
% source: results/phase8b_h16_ensemble_headline.csv, lstmres_corr_top1_ens vs lstm_own_era5_ens
$+0.9$ to $+2.0\,\%$ at every lead 1--6 (all
$p \le 3.2\times10^{-8}$), beats 20 of 20 degree-matched random-graph
% source: results/phase8_stratification.csv (positive in all 60 stratum x lead cells; resolved x cont_lowmid +0.89..+2.00%)
placebos in every lead--seed cell, and is positive in all 60 strata-by-lead
cells with no concentration in shared-footprint basins -- so the leakage
explanation that fits the linear pocket does not fit it. Short-lead TWSA forecasting is a filtering
problem; beyond two to three months it becomes a forcing problem, and the two
skill sources are complementary rather than competing.
\end{abstract}

\introduction
\label{sec:intro}

The GRACE and GRACE-FO satellite missions have measured monthly changes in
terrestrial water storage (TWS) since 2002, integrating groundwater, soil
moisture, surface water, snow, and ice into a single observable that no in
situ network can replicate \citep{tapley2004grace, landerer2020gracefo}.
Anomalies of this quantity (TWSA) carry months of memory, which makes them
both a target and a source of predictability: TWSA forecasts support drought
early warning and water management \citep{li2026fldas, arsenault2020nhyfas},
flood-potential assessment \citep{reager2014flood}, and the bridging of the
2--3 month latency of GRACE-FO data releases \citep{mo2025nrt}.

A growing literature forecasts TWSA directly from data. Neural networks have
been trained on GRACE and hydrometeorological series at basin scale
\citep{ahmed2019narx}, at grid scale globally \citep{li2020wrr, li2024grl,
li2026fcast}, for Europe with subsequent assimilation \citep{li2025wrr}, and
for latency-filling \citep{mo2025nrt}; statistical time-series methods have
been applied per basin \citep{ahi2021prediction}; benchmark predictability
has been quantified at decadal scale \citep{zhu2019elasticity}; and
physically based systems forecast TWS with seasonal meteorological forcing
\citep{li2026fldas, getirana2020groundwater}. Nearly all of these studies quantify their skill
against some form of persistence or climatology, and the implicit logic is
that whatever beats persistence has extracted predictive structure beyond
simple memory.

This paper starts from an observation that undermines that logic for GRACE
specifically. Basin-average TWSA is not a state variable observed cleanly; it
is a state variable observed through a noisy instrument, with observation
errors that are large for small and arid basins \citep{save2016mascons,
landerer2020gracefo}. A persistence forecast -- last month's observed
anomaly, damped or not -- propagates the observation noise of the anchor
month into every forecast. The proper persistence-class reference for a noisy
observable is not persistence of the observation but persistence of the
\emph{filtered state}: an estimate of the signal with the observation noise
removed. For an AR(1) signal observed with white noise this optimal filter is
the Kalman filter \citep{kalman1960}, and it costs three parameters per
basin. If this stronger member of the persistence class beats standard
persistence by several percent, then part of the reported skill of learned
models over persistence is not learned structure at all -- it is noise
filtering that a two-line state-space model performs for free.

The same benchmark-hygiene argument has been made in sea-ice forecasting,
where \citet{niraula2021seaice} showed that damped-persistence references
that ``do not exploit observations optimally'' flatter dynamical forecast
systems, and built a stronger persistence-class benchmark from spatial
damping. We execute the analogous move for satellite-observed TWSA via
explicit signal/observation-noise separation. Within the GRACE literature,
Kalman machinery is well established but serves different purposes: ensemble
Kalman filters assimilate TWSA into land-surface models
\citep{springer2026review}, and state-space smoothers densify or clean the
gravity-field record itself \citep{kankanige2026persistence}. Neither uses a
univariate AR(1)-plus-observation-noise model on the finished basin series
\emph{as a forecast benchmark}, which is the role it plays here; our filter
is not a data-assimilation system and updates no model states. Related in
spirit, \citet{ahi2021prediction} applied exponential-smoothing family
methods per basin without a noise-separation argument, and
\citet{kankanige2026persistence} found persistence-based prediction of GRACE
spherical-harmonic coefficients competitive at sub-monthly scales --
independent support that the persistence class is underrated. Most directly,
\citet{nie2025linear} showed on 515 basins that deep architectures do not
beat linear regression for TWS prediction when autoregressive information is
deliberately excluded from the feature set; we show the complementary result
that when autoregressive information is \emph{included}, how one filters it
dominates the model comparison.

If short-lead skill is mostly filtering, what is left for models to earn?
The seasonal hydrologic forecasting literature answered a structurally
identical question long ago: skill at short leads comes from initial
conditions, and skill at long leads from meteorological forcing, with a
crossover in between \citep{wood2008esp, shukla2011seasonal, koster2010glace2,
yossef2013skill, pechlivanidis2020drivers}. That partition was established
with physically based models in ESP/reverse-ESP experiments, mostly for
streamflow and soil moisture. It has not previously been located on
satellite-observed TWSA, nor measured between two real, published,
data-driven forecast systems. We do both, comparing our
observation-noise-aware system head-to-head with the GRACE-FCast hindcasts of
\citet{li2026fcast}, whose LSTM ingests exogenous hydrometeorological
predictors and, by design, no GRACE lags. The comparison locates an empirical
crossing at approximately two months. We describe it as consistent with the
initial-conditions/forcing partition rather than as a decomposition of skill
into sources: the two systems differ along many axes at once, so what is
measured is the lead profile, not the effect of any single design choice.

The third question the paper answers is whether TWSA fields themselves carry
usable cross-basin information -- whether one basin's history helps forecast
another's future once own-basin filtering and local meteorology are accounted
for. Spatial and graph-based TWSA methods increasingly assume so:
\citet{li2026fcast} shift predictors across space, \citet{arzoumanidis2026stgnn}
build a proximity-plus-correlation basin graph for TWSA reconstruction, and
\citet{steidl2025hignn} forecast TWSA with a hierarchical basin-graph network.
None of these works isolates the incremental contribution of the cross-basin
information, and none tests the graph against a null; \citet{steidl2025hignn}
additionally target a reconstructed rather than observed TWSA product. To
our knowledge, no prior work measures the incremental cross-basin information
in \emph{observed} GRACE TWSA against null models. We do so with
degree-matched random graphs, a mascon leakage distance exclusion, a
resolution stratification, and full ERA5 local-meteorology conditioning. The
answer is two-sided, and the two sides must be kept apart: at the linear
tier the incremental effect is a null, while the same neighbor signal does
deliver skill through a dedicated nonlinear correction stage. We therefore
also show that the \emph{delivery mechanism} decides whether the signal is
usable by a deep model at all.

The contributions, in order:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{An observation-noise-aware benchmark.} A per-basin
three-parameter Kalman filter (AR(1) signal plus observation noise) beats

damped persistence by 5.0--8.8\,\% at leads 1--2 and per-basin ridge

the strongest own-basin reference at every lead. Consequence: benchmarked
against
persistence, a substantial share of published ``ML skill'' on deseasonalized
TWSA is reproduced by three-parameter state-space filtering alone. We frame this

of that forcing, and a small spatial correction -- is a map of where genuine
information lives.
\item \textbf{The initial-conditions/forcing crossover, located on observed

short leads are a filtering problem, long leads a forcing problem.
\item \textbf{Cross-basin information: a linear null, a nonlinear signal, and

\emph{At the linear tier it is a null.} The registered capacity-matched

\emph{leakage pocket}, not a size effect: splitting basins by how much of
their mascon-weighted signal comes from land outside the basin isolates the

basins GRACE resolves ($+1.16\,\%$, $p = 0.0045$) and among smaller ones

invisible to standard linear methods and to naive deep-model inputs; we
exhibit a delivery that finds it, a null-model control showing that what
is found is the neighbor's \emph{information} rather than the two-stage
architecture, and a stratification showing it is not the footprint-sharing
artifact that explains the linear pocket -- a delivery result we connect to
gradient starvation in joint training \citep{pezeshki2021gradient}.

\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{GRACE/GRACE-FO mascons and basin sample}
\label{sec:data_grace}

We use CSR RL0603M mascon solutions \citep{save2016mascons} on a
$0.25^{\circ}$ grid, spanning April 2002 through May 2026, expressed as
equivalent water height (cm); the distributed file is titled RL0603M and
reports product version RL06.2.
% source: raw NetCDF global attrs (title "CSR GRACE and GRACE-FO MASCON RL0603M"; product_version RL06.2), verified 2026-08-13
Each of the 257 monthly solutions is assigned to its official calendar
month using the product's documented missing-month list, with the solution's
data span asserted to overlap its assigned month. Midpoint binning
mislabels the November 2011 and May 2015 solutions, whose arcs are centered
on 31 October 2011 and 27 April 2015; under that convention two months hold
two solutions each and November 2011 and May 2015 are silently dropped from
the record. This was a real defect in an earlier version of our pipeline and
is fixed here.
% source: audit 2026-08-13 P0-1 and results/post_rerun_audit.md sect. 6 (solutions 110/144; only 2 of 257 differ); src/gracefc/basins.py assign_solution_months
Basins are defined by a HydroSHEDS-based Level-3 basin--mascon partition
\citep{lehner2013hydrosheds}; each basin series is the cosine-latitude
area-weighted mean of its mask cells, with longitude centroids computed by
circular mean. From the 284 basins in the partition we exclude 46 ice-sheet
% source: memory/grace-twsa-project.md; docs/STUDY_CONTEXT.md ("284 -> 46 ice-sheet + 4 water-body -> 234")
basins (Antarctica, Greenland) and 4 water bodies, leaving \textbf{234
basins} on all inhabited continents; 38 of these are African (37 mainland
plus Madagascar), and 8 are glaciated and tracked as a separate stratum.
% source: docs/STUDY_CONTEXT.md (Africa count); results/phase2_strata.csv (stratum value_counts: glaciated 8, verified 2026-08-15)

\paragraph{Basin areas and native mascon geometry.} Basin areas used in the
stratifications of Sect.~\ref{sec:results_neighbor} are our own
cosine-latitude integration of the mask file. Native mascon assignment comes
from CSR's official ancillary products: the RL06.3 native-mascon-ID mapping
file, which labels every $0.25^{\circ}$ grid cell with its native mascon, and
the RL06 v02 land/ocean masks \citep[doi:10.15781/cgq9-nh24;][]{save2016mascons},
which CSR recommends for basin definitions to minimize coastal leakage.
% source: results/csr_geometry_validation.csv; data/raw/csr_ancillary/; https://www2.csr.utexas.edu/grace/RL06_mascons.html (mapping file + v02 masks + coastal-leakage recommendation, verified 2026-08-15)
As an internal check we also recovered the tiles empirically -- the gridded
product replicates one tile value across the $0.25^{\circ}$ cells it covers,
so fingerprinting eight months and grouping cells on exact series equality
reconstructs the partition -- and the two agree \emph{exactly}: 42{,}107
tiles in both, cell-level purity 1.0 in both directions, adjusted Rand index
1.0, and per-basin contamination values identical to four decimals.
% source: results/csr_geometry_validation.csv (42,107 = 42,107; purity 1.0/1.0; ARI 1.0); results/rerun_resolution_official.log (contamination Spearman 1.0000, max abs diff 0.0000)
The official tiles have median area 12{,}123\,km$^2$ (IQR 11{,}518--13{,}032),
with cell counts rising from 16 to 24 with latitude -- near-constant area at
varying cell count, CSR's ${\sim}1^{\circ}$ equal-area geodesic grid. A
90{,}000\,km$^2$ basin therefore spans roughly \emph{seven} native mascons,
not one; only 3 of the 234 basins span fewer than two. We accordingly do not
describe that threshold as one resolution element. CSR separately advises
caution for basins below approximately 200{,}000\,km$^2$, a cut under which
35 of our 199 nominally resolved basins fall; Sect.~\ref{sec:results_neighbor}
therefore repeats the footprint stratification at that threshold as a
sensitivity.
% source: resolution_diagnostics.csv (199 basins >=90k, 35 of them <200k); CSR RL06.3 page "exercise caution ... basins smaller than approximately 200,000 km^2"

as the TWSA aggregation, mapping each $0.25^{\circ}$ mask cell to its nearest
$0.5^{\circ}$ ERA5 cell. The resulting table (284 basins $\times$ 305
months) is gapless.
% source: results/RUN_LOG.md, era5 ingestion entry (284 x 305, gapless)

For the head-to-head comparison we use the public CSR-FCast hindcast of

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Target definition: fold-safe deseasonalization}
\label{sec:methods_target}

For each basin and each evaluation fold we fit, on the training window only,
a climatology consisting of a linear trend plus annual and semiannual
harmonics (six coefficients, ordinary least squares). The frozen
coefficients deseasonalize both training and test data; the residual is
divided by its training-window standard deviation. The forecast target is
this deseasonalized, per-basin-standardized residual -- the interannual
anomalies that drought and flood applications need -- and all
pooled metrics are computed in this standardized space so that high-variance
basins do not dominate.
% source: memory/grace-twsa-project.md (deseasonalized target definition)
Physical-unit (cm) results are reported where noted.

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:methods_eval}

\paragraph{Folds.} Five expanding-window folds whose issue windows jointly
cover June 2019 through May 2026 (17, 17, 17, 17, and 15 issue months per
basin at lead 1, one fewer per basin at each successive lead as the shifted
target runs off the end of the record). The matched sample is therefore
% source: results/post_rerun_audit.md sect. 1 and phase8_corrected_audit.md sect. 5 (n identical for every model at each horizon)
$n = 19{,}422 / 19{,}188 / 18{,}954 / 18{,}720 / 18{,}486 / 18{,}252$ rows at
leads 1--6, identical for every model at a given lead.
Fold membership is defined on forecast \emph{issue} dates: within each fold,
every climatology, filter parameter, model coefficient, neighbor graph, and
standardization moment is fit strictly on observations before the fold's
first issue date, and every test forecast is issued at or after that date --
the model is frozen once and then used forward, as it would be in
deployment. (An earlier design split on target dates, which let lead-2--6
transforms see up to $h-1$ months past the issue date; a 2026-08-13 audit
caught this and the entire pipeline was rerun under the issue-date
protocol.)
Earlier issue windows are unusable: the 2017--2018 gap between GRACE and
GRACE-FO destroys the lag windows needed to forecast across them.
All results are retrospective potential skill: issue time is defined
relative to the last available observation, so the 2--3 month GRACE-FO
release latency and ERA5 availability at issue time are not modeled.

\paragraph{Metrics and tests.} The primary metric is pooled RMSE in
standardized units, converted to a skill score $1 -
\mathrm{MSE}_A/\mathrm{MSE}_B$ against a stated reference. Pooled model
comparisons use the Diebold--Mariano test \citep{diebold1995comparing} with
the small-sample correction of \citet{harvey1997testing} on cross-basin
monthly mean loss differentials, with Newey--West HAC variance
\citep{newey1987hac} (maximum lag
$\max(h-1,1)$); confidence intervals use a moving-block bootstrap
\citep{kunsch1989blockboot} (block length $\max(3,h)$, 2000 draws, so the
block covers the lag-$(h{-}1)$ dependence of overlapping $h$-step forecasts).
% source: src/gracefc/stats.py block_bootstrap_skill_ci (block = max(3, horizon), audit repair 2026-08-15)
Per-basin significance uses per-basin DM statistics on the scored test
months -- 83 at lead 1 declining monthly to 78 at lead 6, the last issue
month receding from April 2026 to November 2025 as the shifted target runs
off the record --
% source: results/phase8_corrected_audit.md sect. 5 (issue months 83/82/81/80/79/78 for h1..h6; verified against kalman_predictions.csv 2026-08-15)
with Benjamini--Hochberg false-discovery-rate control at $q=0.10$
\citep{benjamini1995fdr}, following the field-significance reasoning of
\citet{wilks2016stippling}; we always report helped and hurt basins
separately.

\subsection{Baseline ladder and the observation-noise-aware benchmark}
\label{sec:methods_kalman}

The baseline ladder comprises: zero-anomaly climatology; persistence (last
observed anomaly); damped persistence in two variants (an AR(1) damping
coefficient fit per basin, and a regression-estimated variant), of which we
always score against the stronger variant at each lead; a pooled ridge on
own-basin lagged anomalies ($\alpha = 1$, lags 0, 1, 2, 5, 11 months); the
same ridge with climate-index lags; and a per-basin ridge.
% source: results/paper_baseline_ladder.csv (model list); lag set per project feature construction

The benchmark this paper argues for models each basin's deseasonalized
residual $y_t$ as an AR(1) latent signal $x_t$ observed with additive white
noise:
\begin{align}
x_t &= \rho\, x_{t-1} + \epsilon_t, \qquad \epsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, q),\\
y_t &= x_t + \eta_t, \qquad \eta_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, r),
\end{align}
with $(\rho, q, r)$ estimated per basin by maximum likelihood (L-BFGS-B,
multiple starts) on pre-test data.
% source: src/gracefc/kalman.py (fit_kalman_ar1)
The $h$-month forecast is $\hat{y}_{t+h|t} = \rho^{h} x_{t|t}$, damped
persistence of the \emph{filtered state}. The point forecast depends on
$\rho$ and the noise ratio $q/r$ only; the filter handles the mission gap
and missing months by skipping update steps, which inflates state
uncertainty without biasing the state. We call this forecast the
\emph{Kalman forecast}. Two further members of the same ``filtering class''
reuse the filtered state: the \emph{ridge on filtered states}, a pooled
ridge on lagged filtered states, and the neighbor-augmented variants of
Sect.~\ref{sec:methods_neighbors}. We emphasize what this benchmark is not:
it assimilates nothing into any model and is unrelated to the
GRACE data-assimilation literature reviewed by \citet{springer2026review};
it is a forecast reference with three scalars per basin.

\paragraph{Identification caveat, and the $r=0$ ablation.} The decomposition
into signal variance $q$ and observation-noise variance $r$ is a modeling
choice, not a measurement: high-frequency hydrology, non-AR(1) dynamics, and
decomposition error are absorbed into $r$ alongside true instrument noise,
and in a substantial minority of basin--fold fits the likelihood pushes $r$
to its lower boundary: all 1170 basin--fold fits converge, but 259 of them
% source: results/post_rerun_audit.md sect. 6 (kalman_fold_params.pkl diagnostics: 1170/1170 converged, 259 at r<1e-6, 31 basins at the boundary in all five folds)
land at $r < 10^{-6}$, and 31 basins sit at the boundary in all five folds.
So $r$ is not, per basin, a clean instrument-noise estimate. Whether the
benchmark's \emph{margin} is nonetheless the filtering term is a separate,
answerable question, and an ablation answers it: refitting the identical
model class by the identical MLE with $r$ fixed at zero -- which forces the
update gain to one, so the ``filter'' tracks the raw observation and the
model degenerates to damped persistence with MLE $\rho$ -- removes the
entire margin and more. The free-$r$ filter beats its $r=0$ twin by
$+5.4\,\%$ at lead 1 rising to $+12.7\,\%$ at lead 5 (DM $p <
10^{-15}$ at every lead), while the $r=0$ twin \emph{trails} the tuned
damped-persistence baseline at leads 2--6 ($-0.9$ to $-11.6\,\%$): the
AR(1) structure alone, without the noise term, is worth nothing beyond
persistence at any lead past the first.
% source: results/r0_ablation_summary.csv (noise term +5.35/+9.63/+11.67/+12.48/+12.65/+12.27%; rho term +0.90/-0.93/-6.84/-10.75/-11.57/-9.85%; full margin +6.21/+8.79/+5.62/+3.07/+2.55/+3.63%)
What is filtered out doubtless mixes instrument noise with fast unmodelled
hydrology; the ablation attributes the margin to the act of filtering the
observation before extrapolating, not to a specific noise source.

\subsection{Neighbor graphs, controls, and the jump screen}
\label{sec:methods_neighbors}
\label{sec:controls}

\paragraph{Graphs.} For each target basin and fold we select one source
basin (top-1) by: training-window Pearson correlation of the TWSA series
(\emph{correlation graph}, the headline treatment); correlation of the
source's one-month-lagged series with the target (\emph{predictive-lag
graph}); or great-circle proximity (\emph{geographic graph}). Neighbor
information enters as the lagged, forward-propagated filtered state of the
source basin appended to the target's feature set. Distance-restricted
variants exclude sources within 300, 500, or 1000\,km.

\paragraph{Degree-matched random graphs.} This is the primary control
throughout the paper. Every neighbor model is compared
against 20 or 50 placebo graphs that preserve in-degree but draw source basins
uniformly at random. The placebo models are refit end-to-end, so the
comparison is capacity-matched; beating all $N$ draws gives an empirical
rank $p_{\mathrm{rank}} = 1/(N{+}1)$.
% source: results/phase3b_summary.csv (50 draws), results/phase8b_h16_summary.csv (20 draws)
Two properties of this statistic must be kept in view. First,
$p_{\mathrm{rank}}$ is bounded below by $1/(N{+}1)$, so at $N = 20$ every
``significant'' cell is pinned at $0.0476$ and the rank $p$ understates the
evidence; where the margin matters we therefore also report the real graph's
position in placebo standard deviations.
% source: results/phase8_corrected_audit.md sect. 4c (p_rank floor 1/21; z-scores are the real story)
Second, the placebo draws are reused across folds and across horizons, so
per-lead rank results are not independent events (Sect.~\ref{sec:disc_limitations}).

\paragraph{IAAFT surrogates.} Iterated amplitude-adjusted Fourier transform
surrogates \citep{schreiber1996surrogate, schreiber2000surrogate} preserve
each basin's marginal distribution and power spectrum while destroying
cross-basin phase alignment. The transform runs on the full monthly grid
with GRACE gap months linearly interpolated and the gap pattern re-imposed
afterward, so the preserved spectrum belongs to the true monthly time axis
rather than a gap-compressed one. The \emph{real} selected graph is reused
for all 99 surrogate datasets: the tested null is that the selected
neighbor's time alignment carries no information, with selection itself
covered by the random-graph placebos. Surrogates are generated from the full
record, which is conservative (the null draws share the real series'
full-record statistics, and no information flows into the real arm).
% source: scripts/run_phase4_surrogates.py + src/gracefc/surrogates.py (repaired design, audit 2026-08-15)

\paragraph{Conditioning.} Climate-index conditioning gives ENSO and IOD lags
to every model in a comparison; ERA5 conditioning gives all 11 local-meteorology variables
(lags 0--2, deseasonalized, train-standardized, winsorized at $\pm 10\sigma$)
to every model, while placebos re-randomize only the neighbor. The test is
one-directional: if conditioning leaves the neighbor estimate unmoved, the
neighbor is not acting as a proxy for shared local weather. It does not, on
its own, establish that the neighbor carries a signal.
% source: results/phase6_era5_headline.csv (ridge_corr_top1_era5 vs ridge_own_era5); index-conditioning arm pending rerun
The winsorization guard exists because near-zero training variance in arid
basins otherwise detonates standardized ERA5 features (the 2023 Libya flood
reaches several orders of magnitude beyond the training standard deviation).
% source: scripts/run_phase6_era5.py CLIP_SIGMA guard; magnitude diagnostic pending recomputation on the corrected folds

\paragraph{Footprint-sharing stratification.} The physical leakage quantity a
neighbor effect could be an artifact of is not basin size but the degree to
which a basin's mascon footprint reaches beyond the basin. We therefore
define, per basin, a \emph{contamination} score: the share of the basin's
mascon-weighted signal contributed by land outside the basin,
$\sum_t (w_t/W)\,(1 - f_t)$, where $w_t$ is the basin's weight on native tile
$t$ and $f_t$ is that tile's fill fraction from within the basin.
% source: results/RUN_LOG.md 2026-08-15 resolution audit, item 2 (contamination definition; Spearman(area, contamination) = -0.325); results/resolution_diagnostics.csv
Contamination and area are nearly independent axes (Spearman $-0.325$), so
stratifying on one is not a restatement of the other. We report contamination
terciles, the $90{,}000$\,km$^2$ area split retained from earlier drafts, and
the $2\times2$ cross of the two. Two properties of the metric bound its
% source: results/RUN_LOG.md 2026-08-15 resolution audit, caveats (union-of-284-masks land definition; island/coastal basins score LOW)
interpretation: it treats the union of all 284 mask units as ``land'', so the
ocean half of a coastal tile is not counted as foreign and island and coastal
basins score low; it is therefore a \emph{lower bound} on footprint sharing.

\paragraph{Jump screen.} A $6\sigma$ outlier screen flags basins with
step-like jumps and removes them before the neighbor comparison, in a
train-only version and in a test-informed variant kept as a post-hoc
diagnostic only. On the corrected pipeline the train-only screen flags 2
basins (Taiwan; Northern Kordofan) and leaves the lead-1 neighbor comparison
at $+0.21\,\%$ against its own-ridge reference, still ahead of all 50 placebo
draws ($p_{\mathrm{rank}} = 0.02$); the test-informed variant flags 27 and
gives $+0.08\,\%$, likewise 50/50.
% source: results/phase4_jump_screen.csv (train_only_6sigma n=2 skill +0.00207, 50/50, p_rank .0196; full_series_6sigma n=27 skill +0.00082, 50/50)

\subsection{Learned models}
\label{sec:methods_models}

All learned models predict the residual of the Kalman forecast (the final
prediction is the Kalman forecast plus the learned correction), and each is
paired with a ridge ``twin'' on identical features so that architecture and
information content are never conflated. Unless stated otherwise, deep
models are run with multiple seeds and reported as seed ensembles.

\paragraph{Nonlinear heads.} Gradient-boosted trees and multilayer
perceptrons on the same feature sets as the ridges.

\paragraph{Residual MLP.} Stage one: the ridge twin on own-basin
features. Stage two: a small MLP (hidden layers 64--32, early stopping)
trained on the stage-1 \emph{training} residual using only the neighbor's
propagated filtered-state lags. Three seeds.

\paragraph{LSTM.} A shared-encoder LSTM \citep{hochreiter1997lstm} over
12-month windows of the own filtered state concatenated with the 11 ERA5
channels, with a time-ordered ${\sim}85/15$ train/validation split for early
stopping; two seeds. Variants add the neighbor state as a thirteenth input
channel.

\paragraph{The stacked system: LSTM plus a residual-correction stage.} Stage
one: the own-state + ERA5 LSTM above, no neighbor. Stage two: an MLP trained
on the stage-1 training residual from the propagated neighbor state only.
Placebo graphs re-randomize only the stage-2 neighbor and ride the stage-1
network of the matching seed, so the placebo comparison is like-for-like
% source: results/phase8_corrected_audit.md sect. 4b and results/RUN_LOG.md 2026-08-15 (per-seed placebo emission fix)
within each seed. This construction matters: scoring one
seed's model against another seed's placebos introduces a handicap several
times larger than the placebo spread itself.
The same training run also produces a neighbor-free \emph{own-state control},
which sends each basin's \emph{own} propagated state through the identical
stage-2 path -- same stage-1 network object, same stage-2 model, capacity, and
seed -- so that only the information source differs. We report it as a
% source: results/phase8_corrected_audit.md sect. 1 (mechanical fairness verified) and sect. 2c (inferentially too easy; placebo is the primary control)
diagnostic rather than as the primary control, for reasons given in
Sect.~\ref{sec:results_delivery}.

\paragraph{Graph attention network.} A one-layer GAT \citep{velickovic2018gat}
with one- and two-hop variants, three seeds, included to test learned
message passing.

\subsection{Comparison protocol for GRACE-FCast}
\label{sec:methods_li}

The \citet{li2025pangaea} hindcast is aggregated to our basin geometry by
exact $0.25^{\circ}\!\rightarrow\!1^{\circ}$ cell-containment weights; 7
island basins with zero coverage in their land mask drop out, leaving
\textbf{227 matched basins}. Their forecasts are moved into each fold's
deseasonalized standardized space using our train-window climatology plus a
per-(fold, basin, lead) mean offset estimated on pre-test data only. The
matched sample is the identical set of (basin, target-month) rows for every
model at every lead: 60 test months, folds 1--4; fold 5 lies entirely beyond
their final initialization. This gives a complete rectangle of
$227 \times 60 = 13{,}620$ rows per model at each lead.
% source: results/phase8b_li_comparison_headline.csv (n_rows 13620, n_months 60, n_basins 227); results/phase8_corrected_audit.md sect. 6
An independent audit reproduced the aggregation, alignment, and every
headline number digit-for-digit (maximum difference 0.0 across 30 recomputed
cells).
% source: results/phase8_corrected_audit.md sect. 6 (PASS, exact)

Two protocol asymmetries favor their system and are carried as caveats
wherever it wins: their training uses the \citet{yin2023gtws} gap
reconstruction (hindsight information across the 2017--2018 gap), and their
product re-extrapolates seasonal and trend components monthly whereas our
climatology is fold-frozen -- a difference worth about 0.07--0.09 in skill
units at leads 4--6, bounded by their non-seasonal variant, which still wins
those leads.
% source: results/phase8b_li_comparison_headline.csv (lstmres_corr_top1_ens vs li_lstm_full minus vs li_lstm_nonseas at h4-h6: 0.085/0.074/0.070)

\subsection{Cross-basin information at the linear tier: a null result with a footprint-sharing pocket}
\label{sec:results_neighbor}

At the linear tier the cross-basin channel does not pay. Adding one
correlation-selected neighbor's lagged filtered state to the ridge on filtered states
-- the registered, capacity-matched paired comparison, in which both models
share the same head and own-basin features -- changes pooled lead-1 skill by
% source: results/phase3b_predictions.csv via results/post_rerun_audit.md sect. 2 (kalman_corr_top1 vs kalman_own_ridge h1 +0.3076%, DM p=0.1986); CI from results/phase6_era5_headline.csv ridge_corr_top1 vs ridge_own h1
$+0.31\,\%$ (95\,\% CI $-0.09$ to $+0.82$, DM $p = 0.199$) -- not
significant. At leads 2--6 the estimate is negative at every lead
% source: results/post_rerun_audit.md sect. 2 (h2 -0.097, h3 -0.192, h4 -0.358, h5 -0.349, h6 -0.321; all ns, closest p=0.070 at h5 against the neighbor)
($-0.10$, $-0.19$, $-0.36$, $-0.35$, $-0.32\,\%$), also without
significance; the nearest thing to a significant result anywhere in this
family runs \emph{against} the neighbor (lead 5, $p = 0.070$). We report this
as a controlled negative result, and we are explicit that it reverses our own
earlier finding: before the two evaluation defects of
Sect.~\ref{sec:methods_eval} were fixed, the same contrast read $+0.49\,\%$
with $p = 0.022$. What makes the null informative rather than merely
inconclusive is the control battery the estimate was put through
(Table~\ref{tab:controls} and Fig.~\ref{fig:controls}), which no prior
spatial-TWSA claim has faced, and which localizes the little that is there.

\paragraph{Two questions that must be kept apart.} The placebo evidence and
the paired-contrast evidence answer different questions, and blurring them is
how a null becomes an apparent finding.
\emph{Does correlation-selection beat a random neighbor?} At lead 1, yes: the
% source: results/phase3b_summary.csv, kalman_corr_top1 h1 (50/50 placebos beaten, p_rank=0.0196); kalman_corr_min300_top1 h1 also 50/50
real graph beats all 50 degree-matched random graphs
($p_{\mathrm{rank}} = 0.0196$), and so does the $\ge$300\,km-restricted
variant. The selection step is doing something real.
\emph{Does adding a neighbor beat not adding one?} No ($p = 0.199$). The
first question is about graph construction; only the second is about skill,
and only the second is the paper's claim. Beyond lead 1 both answers turn
% source: results/post_rerun_audit.md sect. 2 (kalman_corr_top1 h2-h6: 0/50 placebos beaten, p_rank 1.0; min300 arm is 2/50 at h2)
negative together: at leads 2--6 the real graph is beaten by 0 of 50
placebos, meaning the correlation-selected neighbor is worse than a randomly
chosen basin. (The $\ge$300\,km variant reaches 2 of 50 at lead 2; 0 of 50
elsewhere.)

\paragraph{Stratification (primary sensitivity): the effect is a
footprint-sharing pocket, not a size effect.} Splitting the 234 basins at
$90{,}000$\,km$^2$ puts the whole lead-1 estimate in the small stratum
% source: results/post_rerun_audit.md sect. 2 (recomputed from phase3b_predictions.csv, split on basin_meta.csv below_resolution)
(35 basins, $+1.033\,\%$, $p = 0.00065$) and none of it in the large one
(199 basins, $+0.099\,\%$, $p = 0.715$), and that split is not a knife edge:
% source: results/RUN_LOG.md 2026-08-15 resolution audit, item 3a (results/resolution_sweep_area.csv: stable +0.93..+1.34% across 60k-200k cuts; complement never significant)
every cut from $60{,}000$ to $200{,}000$\,km$^2$ gives the small stratum
$+0.9$ to $+1.3\,\%$ and leaves the complement non-significant. It is
tempting to read this as GRACE's resolution limit, and an earlier version of
this paper did. That reading is wrong.

Basin size is a proxy. The physical quantity is \emph{footprint sharing} --
how much of a basin's mascon-weighted signal comes from land outside the
basin (Sect.~\ref{sec:controls}) -- and it is nearly independent of area
(Spearman $-0.325$). Crossing the two axes separates them:

exclusion does not remove shared mascons for such basins, so we rest no
novelty claim on the linear pocket, and we retire the ``small basins''
framing that stood in for it. Accordingly we describe this section as a
characterization with null-model controls, not as isolation and validation of
a signal: the controls tell us what the pooled number is \emph{not} (chance
graph structure, shared weather), the paired test tells us it is not
distinguishable from zero, and the stratification tells us that the little
that is there sits exactly where an instrument artifact would put it. Whether
the same is true of the nonlinear correction is a separate question with a
different answer, and Sect.~\ref{sec:results_delivery} tests it directly.

The controls constrain what the lead-1 estimate could be without ever
lifting it clear of zero. It is not chance graph structure -- the real graph
beats all 50 degree-matched placebos -- and it is not shared local weather,
since full ERA5 conditioning leaves it unmoved. The centroid-distance
exclusions bound its spatial scale (${\sim}300$--1000\,km). But the
footprint-sharing stratification places what is left exactly where an
instrumental explanation predicts it, and a centroid-distance rule between
basin \emph{centroids} does nothing about two basins drawing on the same
mascon. The reading most consistent with the whole battery is that at the
linear tier the neighbor is largely acting as a second view of partly the
same measurement -- which would explain why the estimate is confined to

shared-footprint basins and to leads 1--2, and why it is negative beyond

no effect.

\paragraph{Heterogeneity, and per-basin selection.} A pooled null does not
mean a spatially uniform null: a stable pattern of helped and hurt basins can
average to nothing. Two analyses probe this -- a per-basin FDR map
(Fig.~\ref{fig:map}) and a leave-one-fold-out selection experiment that
applies the neighbor only where it helped in the other four folds. The
latter is a measure of cross-fold stability in per-basin benefit, not an
operational recipe: selection for early folds uses folds lying later in
time, so a deployable version would have to select on past folds only.
On the corrected pipeline the per-basin decomposition confirms the
two-sided structure: 128 of 234 basins nominally favor the neighbor and 21
survive FDR control at $q = 0.10$ -- \textbf{13 helped and 8 hurt}. The
hurt set is not noise: it is the familiar great-river roster (Yenisey,
Paran\'a, Yangtze, Amur) whose large, clean footprints invite spurious
distant neighbors. The helped set skews African and coastal (Niger,
S\~ao Francisco, Rukwa, Namibia, Zambezia, the West Nile Delta).

\subsection{Delivery decides: unreliable as an input channel, consistent as a correction stage}
\label{sec:results_delivery}

This experiment holds the information \emph{source} fixed -- the same
selected neighbor's Kalman-filtered state -- and varies how it is delivered.
One caveat must be stated up front: the two deliveries also differ in
representation. The input-channel arm receives the neighbor's 12-month
filtered-state history as a full encoder channel, while the correction stage
receives a single number per forecast, the neighbor's $\rho^{h}$-propagated
current state. Delivery and representation are therefore confounded in the
headline pair, and we do not call the comparison information-identical; a
representation-matched variant (the correction stage fed the identical
12-month history) is reported alongside it.
% source: src/gracefc/experiment_lstm_combined.py (input arm _state_channel 12-month window vs stage-2 propagated_neighbor_features k=1 scalar; hist12 arm added in the 2026-08-15 repair)

Five properties give the correction its evidential weight.

First, and primarily, \textbf{the random-graph placebo.} This is the control
the claim rests on. Its logic is that a placebo neighbor is a feature the
stage-1 network has never seen -- novel, like the real neighbor -- but with
the wrong identity, so it isolates identity from novelty. The correct
statistic is the \emph{increment} each stacked model adds over its own stage-1
network, compared with 20 random-neighbor corrections riding the matching
stage-1 network. Placebo increments cost only
% source: results/phase8_corrected_audit.md sect. 2b (placebo mean skill by lead: -0.268/-0.134/-0.121/-0.119/-0.103/-0.050%)
$-0.27$ to $-0.05\,\%$ -- a junk-neighbor correction buys nothing and pays
the variance price of a second stage, which the audit's error decomposition
predicts to within a few hundredths of a percent -- while the real increment
% source: results/phase8b_h16_summary.csv (placebo_beaten = 20/20, all 12 lstmres_corr_top1_s{0,1} lead-seed cells)
beats all 20 draws in all twelve lead--seed cells. We report the margin
rather than the rank $p$, which is pinned at its $1/21$ floor: the real correction
% source: results/phase8_corrected_audit.md sect. 4c (z-scores -6.8/-11.1/-9.2/-11.3/-10.8/-18.6 placebo sd)
sits $6.8$ to $18.6$ placebo standard deviations beyond the placebo mean.
Measured against the placebo median rather than against stage 1, the
% source: results/phase8_corrected_audit.md sect. 2c (vs placebo median s0: +0.92/+1.45/+1.39/+1.40/+1.60/+1.96)
increment is $+0.92$ to $+1.96\,\%$ -- two independently constructed
references agreeing to within $0.1$--$0.3$ percentage points at every lead.
An earlier version of this test showed a single blemish (16 of 20 at lead 2
for seed 1); it was an artifact of scoring a seed-1 model against placebos
% source: results/phase8_corrected_audit.md sect. 4b and RUN_LOG.md 2026-08-15 placebo-fix rerun (all 12 cells 20/20 after seed-matched placebos)
riding the seed-0 network, and it disappeared once each seed was scored
against its own placebos. One accounting caveat, which we do not minimise: the 20 draws are
reused across all five folds and all six horizons, so the twelve cells are
correlated re-scorings of 20 realizations, not twelve independent events.

Second, \textbf{the own-state control, a diagnostic rather than the primary control.}
This neighbor-free variant feeds each basin's \emph{own}
propagated state through the identical feature path, same stage-1 network,
same stage-2 model, capacity, and seed. Mechanically it is exact: the
% source: results/phase8_corrected_audit.md sect. 1a and 1c (max|fs - kalman| = 0.000e+00; 0 of 234 basins lack a top-1 neighbor, 0 zero-filled rows in either arm)
self-graph reproduces each basin's own propagated state to
$\max|\Delta| = 0.000\times10^{0}$, and no zero-fill asymmetry exists between
the two variants, since all 234 basins have a top-1 neighbor in every fold. The control
% source: results/phase8b_h16_ensemble_headline.csv, lstmres_own_ens vs lstm_own_era5_ens (-1.16/-1.46/-1.90/-2.12/-1.84/-1.80%, p<=2.5e-7)
actively \emph{harms}: $-1.16$ to $-2.12\,\%$ across leads
($p \le 2.5\times10^{-7}$). We use it as evidence that the stage-2 head has
ample capacity to do damage, so the stacked system's gain is not a capacity
artifact, and as evidence for the in-sample residual caveat below. We do
\emph{not} use the neighbor-versus-own-state contrast as a skill number or as
an ``architecture term'', because the control is too easy: its correction is
% source: results/phase8_corrected_audit.md sect. 2a (corr(c,e) = -0.08..-0.16 for the own-state arm; only 0.16-0.54pp of the harm is variance cost, so 79-85% is wrong-direction correction)

in-sample residuals. A control that is actively harmful inflates any contrast
drawn against it.

correction is worth $+0.91$ to $+1.96\,\%$ against its own stage-1 network
and $+0.92$ to $+1.96\,\%$ against the placebo median. Independent
constructions landing on the same interval is the strongest single property
of this result.

``Positive everywhere'' is not the same as ``the same everywhere'', and along
the size axis it is not. Small basins gain \emph{least}, and the gradient is real
rather than visual: the size interaction is significant at every lead
(lead-1 $p = 0.026$, lead-6 $p < 0.001$). We state that plainly because its

distinguishable at any lead

The linear pocket and the stacked correction therefore have
different signatures: one is confined to shared footprints and to leads 1--2,
the other is positive in every stratum at every lead and, if anything, weakest
where the artifact would be strongest. \textbf{Under this stratification the
correction shows no detectable concentration where leakage would put it, and
the size gradient runs opposite to the artifact's prediction.} That is the
strongest statement the design supports: the contamination metric is a
lower-bound proxy (it counts only foreign land), the interaction test is a
null result rather than proof of absence -- with the lead-1 point estimate
trending toward more gain under high sharing ($p = 0.099$) -- and the
stratification reuses the headline's test months. Ruling on footprint sharing
aside, the analysis does not identify what the neighbor is contributing, and
we make no claim that it is a hydroclimatic teleconnection.

Why does the same neighbor signal succeed as a correction and fail to
deliver reliably as an
input? The pattern matches gradient starvation
\citep{pezeshki2021gradient}: in joint training, a strong feature set (the
forcing sequence) starves the gradient signal of a weak, partially
correlated feature (the neighbor state), so the weak channel is either
ignored or fit to noise. A dedicated residual stage gives the weak feature
its own objective. The residual-MLP experiments of
Sect.~\ref{sec:results_neighbor} show the same delivery principle at the
GRACE-only information set, where a neighbor-only residual MLP over the plain

the cross-basin signal is there, and it is only reachable through a stage
that is given nothing else to fit.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the benchmark result means, and does not mean}
\label{sec:disc_benchmark}

The benchmark result is an argument about reference classes, not a new
forecasting method. Persistence-type references are supposed to represent
``no information beyond memory''; for a noisy observable they leave the
persistence class's own headroom unexploited, and that headroom
on deseasonalized GRACE basin series is 5.0--8.8\,\% at the leads where most
% source: results/paper_baseline_ladder.csv, kalman_ar1 h1-h2 (+4.98, +8.79)
data-driven papers claim their skill. Any study that benchmarks against
persistence, damped persistence, or climatology on observed TWSA -- i.e.,
most of the literature cited in Sect.~\ref{sec:intro} -- overstates the
information its model has extracted by roughly this margin, whatever mixture
of measurement error and unmodeled dynamics the filter is exploiting
(Sect.~\ref{sec:methods_kalman}). The fix costs
three parameters per basin. This is the same benchmark-hygiene move that
\citet{niraula2021seaice} made for the sea-ice edge via spatial damping,
executed here via signal/noise separation, and it converges with three
independent lines: persistence-class methods underrated at the
coefficient level \citep{kankanige2026persistence}, statistical per-basin
prediction competitive at basin level \citep{ahi2021prediction}, and linear
models unbeaten when autoregressive information is excluded
\citep{nie2025linear}. Our contribution to that conversation is causal
attribution: with autoregressive information included, what separates the
models the field builds is mostly how well they filter it -- and the $r=0$
ablation makes the attribution an intervention rather than an
interpretation, since deleting only the noise term deletes the margin
(Sect.~\ref{sec:methods_kalman}).

Two scope notes. The argument applies to \emph{observed} TWSA; targets that
are themselves model output, reconstructions, or gap fills (e.g., the
HydroGlobe target of \citealp{nie2025linear}, or the products of
\citealp{humphrey2019gracerec, yin2023gtws, mo2022gap}) contain no satellite
observation noise to filter, so neither the benchmark inflation nor its fix
transfers. And our filter is not data
assimilation \citep{springer2026review}: nothing is assimilated into any
model, and no state but the basin's own scalar signal is estimated.

\subsection{The crossover as a design map}
\label{sec:disc_crossing}

The initial-conditions-versus-forcing partition is old
\citep{wood2008esp, shukla2011seasonal, koster2010glace2, yossef2013skill,
pechlivanidis2020drivers}; what is new is its location on satellite-observed
TWSA, measured between two published data-driven systems with paired tests.
The crossover at ${\sim}2$ months is earlier than for streamflow in
snow-dominated regions but consistent with TWSA's strong memory being
concentrated in exactly the component the filter extracts. The practical
reading is a design map, not a contest: below the crossover, invest in
filtering the initial condition (three parameters suffice; further
architecture buys little); above it, invest in exogenous forcing and in
nonlinear temporal integration of the forcing history -- our own evidence
(Sect.~\ref{sec:results_era5}) shows a ridge on forcing lags cannot carry
ERA5 skill past lead 1, while a sequence model carries it to lead 4, and
\citet{li2026fcast}'s system, which commits fully to this side, carries
forcing-driven skill to lead 6 and beyond. That the linear ERA5 gain now
stops at lead 1 sharpens rather than weakens the map: the useful part of
forcing information at longer leads is not in the lags themselves but in
how a sequence model integrates them.
At basin level the two edges are uncorrelated (Spearman $-0.141$ between
GRACE-FCast's lead-4 edge and the neighbor benefit), so the complementarity
holds across basins as well as across leads.
% source: results/phase6_basin_analysis_q4.csv (spearman_dm_p3b_vs_li_h4 = -0.141)
A genuinely hybrid product -- filtered
initial conditions, forcing-driven long leads, and a spatial correction --
is the obvious system nobody has built; our splice is a descriptive sketch
of its headroom, not that product.

\subsection{The neighbor signal in context}
\label{sec:disc_neighbor}

Spatial context is increasingly assumed to help TWSA prediction
\citep{li2026fcast, arzoumanidis2026stgnn, steidl2025hignn}. Our results
discipline that assumption more than they support it, and the two-sided
answer is the contribution. On the discipline side, the linear result is a
% source: results/post_rerun_audit.md sect. 2 (h1 +0.31% p=0.199); results/resolution_cross_2x2.csv (resolved x cont_lowmid -0.500%)
\emph{null}: one correlation-selected neighbor adds $+0.31\,\%$ at lead 1
($p = 0.199$) and nothing at longer leads. Worse for the assumption, what
little sits inside that null has a leakage signature -- it is confined to
basins whose mascon footprint is shared with land outside the basin and to
leads 1--2, and it is \emph{negative} in the 142 resolved, clean-footprint
basins (Sect.~\ref{sec:results_neighbor}). A graph
attention network given identical information never beats a ridge, and
two-hop neighborhoods never beat one-hop. Anyone building a basin graph for
observed TWSA should expect these to be the default outcomes, and should
stratify by footprint sharing before believing a positive one.

On the support side, the signal is not absent -- it is inaccessible to the
standard tools. Two nonlinear, two-stage constructions recover it: a
% source: results/phase7_corrected_analysis.md Q2(a), Q3 (GRACE-only resMLP ens +0.81/+2.15/+2.17, 12/12 cells beat 20/20 placebos)
neighbor-only residual MLP over a plain ridge at the GRACE-only information
% source: results/phase8b_h16_ensemble_headline.csv (+0.91..+1.96% at h1-h6, 20/20 placebos in all 12 cells)
set ($+0.81$ to $+2.17\,\%$) and a neighbor-only correction stage over a deep
own-state\,+\,ERA5 LSTM ($+0.91$ to $+1.96\,\%$ at every lead 1--6). Both
beat their full random-graph placebo families in every cell, so what is
recovered is the neighbor's identity and not the extra stage. And the
% source: results/phase8_stratification.csv (positive in all 60 stratum x lead cells; resolved x cont_lowmid +0.89..+2.00%, all p<1e-4)
correction does not inherit the linear pocket's signature: it is positive in
all 60 strata-by-lead cells, shows no statistically distinguishable
difference across footprint-sharing terciles, and is worth $+0.89$ to
$+2.00\,\%$ in precisely the resolved, clean-footprint stratum where the
linear effect is negative. Along the size axis it does vary, in the direction
that helps rather than hurts the reading: the smallest basins -- the ones
leakage would favour most -- gain the least. That rules out the leading
artifact explanation. It does not identify a mechanism, and we advance none:
what the two-stage delivery extracts from a neighbor basin's history remains
an open question, and calling it a teleconnection would be an overclaim.
\citet{steidl2025hignn} forecast with a
10-nearest basin graph but on a reconstructed target, with no
persistence-class baselines, no isolation of the graph's contribution, and no
null models; our GAT null, control battery, and footprint-sharing
stratification are the coherent companion measurement -- the graph
assumption, tested, yields a linear null with a leakage pocket, a small
nonlinear signal that survives the leakage stratifier, and the finding that
message passing is the wrong way to extract it.

A regional reading of where the neighbor channel pays would be valuable in a
water-security context (Fig.~\ref{fig:complementarity}), particularly the
hypothesis that spatial TWSA information matters most where reanalysis
forcing is least trustworthy. The corrected pipeline supports it from three
directions. Per basin, ERA5 gain and conditioned neighbor gain are
anti-correlated (Spearman $-0.220$, $p = 0.0007$, 234 basins). By continent,
the two are mirror images: Africa has the smallest median ERA5 gain
($+0.7\,\%$) and the largest median neighbor gain ($+3.1\,\%$ conditioned;
median per-basin skill $+1.6\,\%$ on the linear tier), while Europe shows
the reverse ($+7.6\,\%$ ERA5, $-1.9\,\%$ neighbor). And pooled Africa
sharpens rather than dilutes under conditioning: the unconditioned African
neighbor effect is $+0.85\,\%$ ($p = 0.15$), unchanged by index
conditioning, but rises to $+1.57\,\%$ ($p = 0.0035$) once all eleven ERA5
variables are aboard -- spatial TWSA information is worth most precisely
where the reanalysis has least to say. The GRACE-only residual-MLP result
points the same way (Sect.~\ref{sec:results_neighbor}).

\subsection{Comparability of published numbers}
\label{sec:disc_comparability}

No published study reports pooled deseasonalized basin RMSE over a global
sample, so no external number is directly comparable to ours; we compare
only against baselines recomputed under our protocol, and we flag two
specific numbers a reader might juxtapose. \citet{mo2025nrt} report median
% source: results/phase8_related_benchmarks.md sect. 2.1 (1.79/2.07/2.26 cm, median grid, full signal)
grid-cell RMSE of 1.79/2.07/2.26\,cm at 1--3 months; that figure is a median
over grid cells of \emph{full-signal} errors (seasonal cycle included) on a
different window, three choices that each shrink it relative to our pooled
deseasonalized basin metric, and only their code -- not their predicted
fields -- is archived, so no matched-protocol comparison is possible.
\citet{li2026fldas} report ${\sim}1$\,cm domain-averaged deseasonalized RMSE
% source: results/phase8_related_benchmarks.md sect. 2.2
for Africa; spatial averaging over the domain removes most variance, and
their basin-level skill is categorical (ROC), not RMSE. We also note the
FLDAS-Forecast hindcast fields are public
(\url{https://ldas.gsfc.nasa.gov/fldas/models/forecast}); we did not add a
third head-to-head because the product is regional (its Africa/Middle East
domain covers ${\sim}37$ of our 234 basins), percentile/ROC-oriented, and
ends in December 2020, overlapping only ${\sim}19$ months of our test
% source: memory/grace-novelty-audit.md (FLDAS hindcast scope: ~37 basins, ends Dec 2020, ~19 months overlap)
targets. Its published conclusion -- that initial-condition persistence
dominates sub-seasonal TWS skill \citep{li2026fldas} -- is the qualitative
claim our benchmark formalizes.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:disc_limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Stage-2 residuals are in-sample.} The stacked correction is
trained on the LSTM's training-set residuals. The \emph{evaluation} is
honest (shared stage-1 network, no test information reaches the MLP), but
the LSTM overfits its training set, so the learned correction is plausibly
attenuated relative to one trained on out-of-fold residuals. This is a
statement of likely direction, not a proof of conservatism. It is also not
% source: results/phase8_corrected_audit.md sect. 2a (own-state control's correction anti-correlated with stage-1 error; 79-85% of its harm is wrong-direction, from the in-sample residual protocol)
harmless: it is the mechanism by which the own-state control learns a
systematically wrong-direction correction (Sect.~\ref{sec:results_delivery}).
An out-of-fold stage-2 residual is the ranked next experiment.
\item \textbf{Placebo draws are shared, so the leads are not independent
events.} The 20 degree-matched random graphs are reused across all five folds
% source: results/phase8_corrected_audit.md sect. 4c (same graph per draw index in every fold and at every horizon)
\emph{and} all six horizons -- draw $k$ is the identical graph everywhere --
so the twelve lead--seed cells of Table~\ref{tab:stack} are correlated
re-scorings of 20 realizations rather than twelve independent tests. The
rank statistic is additionally pinned at its $1/21$ floor, which is why we
report placebo standard deviations instead.
\item \textbf{Controls not yet ported to the stacked system.} The
$\ge$300\,km exclusion and meteorology conditioning that bound the leakage
and shared-climate confounds were run for the linear estimate, not for the
stacked correction; the stacked evidence rests on the seed-matched placebo
graphs alone. Indirect, and disclosed as such.
\item \textbf{Comparison asymmetries with GRACE-FCast.} Their training uses
the \citet{yin2023gtws} reconstruction across the mission gap (hindsight);
their seasonal/trend handling contributes ${\sim}0.07$--0.09 of the
% source: results/phase8b_li_comparison_headline.csv (full vs nonseas gap at h4-h6)
full-product edge at leads 4--6 (bounded by the non-seasonal variant, which
still wins those leads); our lead-1 win partly reflects the Kalman filter
% source: results/RUN_LOG.md phase6 Li entry (caveats incl. "our h1 win partly reflects Kalman tuned to this target definition")
being tuned to exactly this target definition; and the matched sample
excludes fold 5 and seven island basins.
\item \textbf{Splice numbers are post hoc} (Sect.~\ref{sec:results_crossing}):
splice points chosen on the evaluated sample, several variants, no
multiplicity control.
\item \textbf{Sample and era.} One mascon product (CSR); test windows all
lie in the post-2019 climate era, and any leave-one-fold-out selection,
though honest in time, cannot escape that era; per-basin estimates rest on
83 lead-1 months.
\item \textbf{Seed budgets.} Two seeds for the LSTM stages, three for the
residual MLP. Seed spread is comparable to the effects being measured -- the LSTM
% source: results/phase7_corrected_analysis.md Q1 (lstm ERA5 gain s0 +2.35 vs s1 +1.27 at h1) and Q2(b) (resmlp_era5 s2 outlier: -0.06/+0.83/+0.44, sign flip vs s0/s1 at h1)
sequence gain nearly halves from the better to the worse seed, the
ERA5 residual MLP has a seed-2 outlier that flips sign at lead 1, and the
% source: results/phase7_corrected_analysis.md Q3 seed flags (LSTM+ERA5 neighbor increment h2: s0 -1.46 vs s1 +0.71)
LSTM neighbor increment at lead 2 flips sign between seeds. This is why every
headline is a seed ensemble and no single-seed number is reported as a
result.
\item \textbf{The filter's noise parameter is not identified as measurement
noise.} 259 of 1170 basin--fold fits push $r$ to its lower boundary and 31
% source: results/post_rerun_audit.md sect. 6 (1170/1170 converged, 259 at r<1e-6, 31 basins at the boundary in all five folds)
basins sit there in all five folds, and the AR(1)-plus-white-noise
decomposition absorbs unmodeled dynamics into $r$
(Sect.~\ref{sec:methods_kalman}); an $r=0$ ablation and replication on
JPL/GSFC mascons are the concrete checks.
\item \textbf{Post-selection inference.} All architectures were developed
and compared on the same five test folds; the stacked system combines
winners of those comparisons, so its results are exploratory
(Sect.~\ref{sec:results_delivery}) and nominal $p$ values understate total
uncertainty. No untouched GRACE-FO period exists for confirmation within
this record.
\item \textbf{Shared mascon footprints carry what pooled linear neighbor
effect there is.} The footprint-sharing stratification
(Sect.~\ref{sec:results_neighbor}) is the primary sensitivity for the linear
neighbor result, and it localizes the entire pooled estimate in basins whose
mascon footprint reaches outside the basin, independently of basin size.
\item \textbf{The contamination metric is a lower bound, and the
stratifications are not independent samples.} Contamination counts only
% source: results/RUN_LOG.md 2026-08-15 audit-repair entry (official mapping + v02 land mask; identical to the basin-union land definition on this grid)
foreign \emph{land} -- with land defined by CSR's official v02 land mask and
tile assignment by the official mapping file (Sect.~\ref{sec:data_grace}) --
so the ocean fraction of a coastal tile is not counted as foreign and island
and coastal basins score low; true footprint sharing is therefore at least
as large as we measure. The linear $2\times2$ is lead 1 on the
neighbor ridge only; the stacked stratification covers leads 1--6 but reuses the same
test months as the headline, so it is a decomposition of the headline rather
than an independent replication.
\item \textbf{Several controls await regeneration.} The IAAFT surrogate,
climate-index conditioning, jump-screen, alternative-filter, and
per-basin analyses were built and run under the pre-correction
protocol and are being rerun; they are marked in the text and no
pre-correction value is carried forward.
\end{enumerate}

\conclusions
\label{sec:conclusions}

For basin-average, deseasonalized GRACE TWSA, the field's standard
persistence benchmark is weaker than its own reference class: a
three-parameter per-basin state-space filter applied to the same
% source: results/paper_baseline_ladder.csv, kalman_ar1 rows; paper_baseline_contrasts.csv
observations beats damped persistence by 5.0--8.8\,\% at leads 1--2 and
the per-basin ridge -- the best conventional model in our ladder at short
leads -- at five of six leads, at negligible cost. That margin is
unchanged by two evaluation-protocol corrections that we applied to our own
pipeline mid-study, which is the strongest evidence we can offer that it is a
property of the benchmark rather than of our implementation. We propose it as
the default reference for this task, and we read the
consequence constructively: benchmarked properly, short-lead TWSA
forecasting is revealed to be a filtering problem, and the meaningful
question becomes what information beats state-space filtering.

The answer has three parts. Exogenous forcing beats it -- ERA5 predictors
% source: results/phase6_era5_headline.csv (+4.62% h1, p=1.0e-4; h2 ns); results/phase7_corrected_analysis.md Q1 and phase8_corrected_audit.md sect. 3 (LSTM ens gain to h4)
add $+4.6\,\%$ at lead 1, and although a linear head cannot carry that
information beyond one month, sequence modeling of the forcing history
extends the gain to lead 4 -- and a published system built entirely on
exogenous forcing \citep{li2026fcast} overtakes our best system beyond lead
2, locating an empirical crossing consistent with the classic
initial-conditions-versus-forcing crossover on
satellite-observed TWSA at about two months. Cross-basin information is the
% source: results/post_rerun_audit.md sect. 2 (h1 +0.31% p=0.199); results/resolution_cross_2x2.csv (resolved x cont_lowmid -0.500%, n=142); results/phase8_stratification.csv (linear cont_tercile_high h1-h2 only)
two-sided part. At the linear tier it is a null -- $+0.31\,\%$ at lead 1
($p = 0.199$) and nothing at longer leads -- and what little sits inside that
null looks like an instrument artifact: stratifying on how much of a basin's
mascon signal comes from outside the basin places the estimate in
shared-footprint basins at leads 1--2 across the size range, and turns it
negative in the 142 resolved basins with clean footprints. Delivery is what
% source: results/phase8b_h16_ensemble_headline.csv (input channel +0.36% p=0.090 at h1, negative h2-h6; correction stage +0.91..+1.96%, all p<=3.14e-08, max at h4, 20/20 placebos in all 12 cells)
changes the answer: fed to a deep encoder as
an input channel the same neighbor signal yields no robust ensemble gain, while
as a dedicated residual-correction stage it contributes $+0.91$ to
$+1.96\,\%$ at every lead 1--6, beating 20 of 20 degree-matched random-graph
placebos in every lead--seed cell and growing rather than decaying with lead.
Two controls carry that claim, and neither is the leaderboard: the placebo
% source: results/phase8_stratification.csv (positive in all 60 stratum x lead cells; resolved x cont_lowmid +0.89..+2.00%)
family, which makes it a statement about the neighbor's information rather
than about the extra stage, and the footprint-sharing stratification, in
which the correction is positive in all 60 strata-by-lead cells, is worth
$+0.89$ to $+2.00\,\%$ in the very basins where the linear effect is
negative, and is if anything weakest in the small, most footprint-shared
basins that the artifact would favour -- so the artifact
that explains the linear pocket does not explain the correction. What does
explain it remains unidentified.

Short leads are a filtering problem; long leads are a forcing problem; the
spatial signal is small, invisible to linear methods, and recoverable only
through a stage built for it. The obvious next system combines all three, and
the obvious next practice is to benchmark it -- and everything else in this
literature -- against persistence of the
filtered state rather than persistence of the noise.
