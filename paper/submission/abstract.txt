Data-driven forecasts of terrestrial water storage are usually scored by their agreement with the observations, or against competing models, rarely against the storage record they start from. How much skill that record alone supplies is unclear. Persistence, the simplest such reference, carries forward the latest deseasonalized residual, including whatever transient part it contains. We test whether filtering the Gravity Recovery and Climate Experiment (GRACE) and GRACE Follow-On (GRACE-FO) record before extrapolation gives a stronger reference. For each basin we fit a Kalman filter with a first-order autoregressive state and additive model noise, and forecast from the filtered state. The split between state and noise is statistical and does not separate physical variability from observation error. Forecasts are evaluated on deseasonalized residuals over 234 basins and five expanding-window test periods in 2019–2026. On the Center for Space Research (CSR) mass concentration (mascon) product the filter reduces mean squared error by 5.0 % at a 1-month lead and 8.8 % at 2 months, against the stronger of two damped-persistence references. Refitting with zero model-noise variance removes that 1-month gain. On the Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) product the filter improves on damped persistence at 2–6 months, but the 1-month difference is not significant. On CSR, a ridge regression on 12 months of filtered storage and ERA5-Land variables lowers standardized error by a further 7.6 % at 1 month, although its pooled error in centimeters does not differ significantly from the filter's. None of the nonlinear models tested on the same inputs has lower error than this ridge at 1 to 3 months. Both the filter and damped persistence have lower standardized error at 1 month than GRACE-FCast, a published deep-learning hindcast, when each is scored on the same mascon product and on the fully covered basins. GRACE-FCast has significantly lower error than the filter from 3 to 5 months onward, depending on product and variant. We therefore recommend scoring storage forecasts against a filtered reference and reporting their errors in physical as well as standardized units.
