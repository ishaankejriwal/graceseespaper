Data-driven forecasts of terrestrial water storage are usually scored by their agreement with the observations, or against competing models, rarely against the storage record they start from. How much skill that record alone supplies is unclear. Persistence, the simplest such reference, carries forward the latest observed anomaly and its measurement error. We test whether filtering the Gravity Recovery and Climate Experiment (GRACE) and GRACE Follow-On (GRACE-FO) record before extrapolation gives a stronger reference. For each basin we fit a Kalman filter with a first-order autoregressive state and additive observation noise, and forecast from the filtered state. Forecasts are evaluated on deseasonalized anomalies over 234 basins and five expanding-window test periods in 2019–2026. On the Center for Space Research (CSR) mascon product the filter reduces mean squared error by 5.0 % at a 1-month lead and 8.8 % at 2 months, against the stronger of two damped-persistence references. Refitting with zero observation-noise variance removes that 1-month gain. On the Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) product the filter improves on damped persistence at 2–6 months; the 1-month difference is not significant. On CSR, a ridge regression on 12 months of filtered storage and ERA5-Land variables lowers standardized error by a further 7.6 % at 1 month, although its pooled error in centimeters is higher than the filter's. No nonlinear model tested has lower error than this ridge at 1–3 months. Both the filter and damped persistence have lower error than the forcing-driven GRACE-FCast hindcast at 1 month. GRACE-FCast has significantly lower error than the filter from 3–5 months onward. Storage forecasts should be scored against a filtered reference and in physical units.
