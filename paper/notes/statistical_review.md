# Statistical and scientific review, 18 September 2026

This review checks the manuscript against the local results and analysis code. It does not establish publication readiness or independently validate the source satellite products.

## Numerical checks

- Recomputed all 66 CSR benchmark-ladder rows from the stored prediction files, using `scripts/build_paper_ladder.py` to load matched observations and the implemented Diebold–Mariano statistic. All RMSEs, MSE skill scores, and p values agree with `results/paper_baseline_ladder.csv`. Maximum absolute RMSE discrepancy was 4.44e-16; maximum p-value discrepancy was 1.00e-16.
- Table B1's small p values are not zero. Reporting values below 0.001 as `<0.001` is consistent with the saved numerical results. Each p value tests equality of forecast loss in either direction. For example, climatology has a very small p value because it is worse than the reference, whereas KF has a very small p value because it is better.
- The pooled tests use the monthly mean loss differential across basins, not all basin-months as independent replicates. Thus the principal CSR comparison at one month has 83 temporal observations, despite 19,422 scored basin-months. This distinction is important in captions and interpretation.
- `scripts/audit_paper_statistics.py` provides a read-only repeat of the numerical check. It also checks printed RMSE/p-value pairs if the original two-row CSR appendix layout remains in use.

## Corrections recommended for this draft

1. **High: area and variance weighting.** Pooled centimeter error is not weighted by basin area. Replace “large/largest basins dominate” with “basins with larger storage variability contribute more to pooled squared error.” Standardization gives equal basin scaling, not equal realized error contribution.
2. **High: percentages in the ablation.** Skill percentages do not multiply. Replace the statement that +5.35% and -0.40% multiply with: “The corresponding MSE ratios multiply: 0.9465 × 1.0040 ≈ 0.9502, recovering the 4.98% improvement over damped persistence.”
3. **High: interpretation of weaker references.** Comparing different denominators does not support an additive “overstatement” of skill by 5–9% and a further 16–23%. The filter demonstrates predictive skill available from the storage history under this study's evaluation. Results from another study cannot be quantitatively corrected by these margins without matched forecasts and targets.
4. **High: causal interpretation of the GRACE-FCast crossover.** The external comparison changes architecture, training, target decomposition, and inputs together. It cannot isolate the contribution of forcing history. Describe the longer-lead advantage as consistent with complementary information in antecedent forcing; do not assert that it measures information absent from storage history.
5. **Medium: persistence through gaps.** Persistence can carry the last observation across missing months; elapsed-time damping can also be defined. KF additionally propagates state uncertainty and accepts a new observation through its update. Remove the claim that persistence has no last observation during the mission gap. Gap forecasts were not evaluated here.
6. **Medium: operational latency.** A one-month observation-relative hindcast is not a three- to four-month real-time forecast. Correct wording: “Predicting next month from an observation released two to three months late requires an observation-relative lead of roughly three to four months.”
7. **Medium: retrospective reference selection.** `build_paper_ladder.py` chooses the stronger damped variant using pooled test RMSE at each horizon. State that this is a retrospective comparison reference, not an operational model-selection rule. Both individual variants remain reported, which makes the choice inspectable.
8. **Medium: statistical significance.** State that DM tests are two-sided and pooled p values are unadjusted across models and horizons. BH adjustment applies to per-basin comparisons. A small p value alone does not indicate improvement. A nonsignificant result (for example p=0.60 on JPL at one month) is not evidence of equivalence; use “no statistically detectable difference” instead of “tie.”
9. **Medium: the two-variance threshold.** Requiring 12 GRACE-FO observations is an implementation rule, not a proved identifiability boundary. Say the model requires at least 12 observations before fitting a separate variance and otherwise falls back to one variance.
10. **Medium: concentration of gains.** “Confined to low-variance basins” is stronger than the stratification supports. “Concentrated in low-variance basins” agrees with the body of the manuscript and avoids an unsupported universal statement.
11. **Medium: mechanism language.** Refitting at r=0 also re-estimates rho and q. The ablation evaluates allowing an observation-noise term within this model family; it does not physically identify measurement noise. Preserve the existing limitation that r may absorb fast hydrology and model misspecification.
12. **Low: early windows and forecast families.** The mission gap makes the selected common lag windows incomplete, but it does not prohibit every possible earlier evaluation. Say earlier windows were excluded under this common-sample design. Likewise, replace claims about “every model” or “every observation-based forecast” with the actual compared model set where the evidence comes from selected external-comparison rows.

## Limits of this audit

The implemented HAC truncation at max(h−1, 1) and moving-block bootstrap length max(3, h) address forecast overlap but do not establish that longer dependence is absent. Bandwidth/block-length sensitivity would be useful before submission for borderline claims. This review has not changed statistical estimates or rerun model training. Other publication requirements, including a final archived data/code DOI and citation verification, remain separate from numerical consistency.
